### A Brief Introduction!
In this course we focus on AI techinques specific to video games, and thus diverting a bit from the typical approach to AI we usually see in a regular AI course. That is, rather than focusing on determining optimal / as close to optimal solutions as possible, we'd be more focused on creating fun and appropriately challenging experiences. These might often lead to approaches that don't lead to optimality in our solutions! For example: The goal is not always to beat a player but rather create an apt amount of challenge (and eventually be overcome by the player).

Another issue with video games is that resources (memory / computation) are often very limited and at odds with other components of the video game, such as graphics/audio/physics simulation etc. Hence, leaving a rather small slice of available computation for AI.

Furthermore players might come in with certain expectations of what an AI agent should be able to do based on the context of the game (constraints), thus leading to issue of fairness / perception of fairness.

Thus, making Game AI different than classic AI, where one might use all information available. Hence, the techniques we go over in this couse may seem familiar, but the way in which we leverage them might be different / new.

### Books to Refer
1. [**AI for games (3rd edition)** by *Ian Millington*](https://learning.oreilly.com/library/view/ai-for-games/9781351053280/): majority of the course that is leveraging a book will come from this one.
2. **Programming game AI by example** by *Matt Buckland*: In particular, related to fuzzy logic.
3. **Computational geometry in C (2nd Edition)** by *Joseph O'Rourkee*: Useful for understanding how to work with geometry in the context of different algorithms, especially useful for robust handling of things like spatial partitioning or space discretization so that we can apply these different AI techniques like say path finding.
